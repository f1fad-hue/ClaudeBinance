Correlated transmission

The same six drivers do not hit the four sleeves the same way. This is where the composite becomes an allocation.

SGOV
Tailwind
A tightening-biased Fed is the one regime where a 0–3 month bill sleeve is paid to wait. Its 3.63% SEC yield against 3.4% headline CPI roughly holds real value at near-zero duration — and every hawkish surprise that hurts the other three sleeves raises its reinvestment rate.
QQQ
Two-sided
The strongest single beneficiary of the capex and productivity boom the Fed is describing — and the sleeve most exposed to the 10-year at 4.78%. Long-duration equity discounts back at a rising rate. Earnings support is real; the multiple is the risk.
IEMG
Tailwind
Captures the fastest-growing bloc — the IMF has China at 4.4% and India at 6.3% for 2026, together 43.6% of all global growth — at 11.6× forward earnings against the Nasdaq-100’s 25.2×. A Fed on hold rather than hiking caps dollar strength, historically the single biggest swing factor for EM returns in dollar terms.
BMNR
Headwind
A levered ETH proxy at the wrong end of every driver: tightening policy drains the liquidity crypto beta feeds on, and a 0.65 correlation to QQQ means it fails precisely when the equity sleeve fails. Convexity, not diversification.
The core tension. Growth is genuine and broad, but it is being met by tightening policy, a rising long end and record-cheap volatility. That argues for staying invested at a full 10-year horizon while shifting the equity mix toward the cheaper, faster-growing bloc — and holding a real cash reserve to buy the repricing rather than to avoid it. Re-scoring every driver against primary sources moved two of the six, and left the composite unchanged at 4.8.

Term structure math

Volatility scales with the square root of time: σh = σann × √(h/12). From VIX 14.32 the implied S&P moves are:

Where this understates risk. Square-root scaling assumes constant volatility and normal returns. Both fail in stress: volatility clusters, and equity tails are fatter than lognormal. At VIX 14.32 the market is pricing a 12-month band of ±23.6% against a 2016–2023 average of 18.9 — implied volatility is roughly 24% cheap to its own history heading into a 15–16 September FOMC where three voters already dissented for a hike and two more said they would have.

Ten-year regime view

Over a decade a portfolio of this composition should expect to live through two to three volatility regime shifts. QQQ's own history sets the honest bound on what the equity sleeve can do:

2000–02 dot-com≈−83%
2007–09 financial crisis−50 to −53%
2020 COVID crash≈−27%
2022 rate-hiking cycle−33 to −37%

Published figures vary by a few points depending on price versus total-return basis and intraday versus closing marks, so these are ranges rather than points. The 2022 episode is the closest analogue to the present configuration — a rate-driven de-rating rather than a credit event — and the −40% QQQ assumption deliberately sits beyond its worst reading, since the starting multiple today is higher. The dot-com figure is the reminder that concentration risk in this index has no theoretical floor, and the reason the equity core is split across two blocs rather than held in one.

Rationale

Start from the horizon, not the headlines. Ten years is long enough that cash drag compounds into a serious cost — SGOV’s $13,649 terminal value against IEMG’s $23,544 on the same $10,000 is the price of caution. The macro signal is therefore spent on which equities to own, not on how much equity to own. Total risk assets stay at 75% in both portfolios.
The equity rotation follows three independent signals. The regional rankings put Asia/EM ahead of the US at every horizon. The valuation data puts EM at 11.6× forward against the Nasdaq-100’s 25.2×, a 40% discount to developed markets versus a 25% long-run norm. The building-block model puts IEMG 0.76 points ahead of QQQ net of fees. All three point the same way, which is why the rotation is 15 points rather than a token tilt.
IEMG is the rare sleeve that improves both sides. Forecast 8.94% net against QQQ’s 8.18%, on lower volatility (18% vs 21%), a shallower drawdown (−33% vs −40%) and half the fee. Adding it raises return and cuts risk simultaneously — which is why the optimization requires no trade-off at all.
Where the model was overruled. Searching every 5%-increment allocation, the highest return-per-unit-risk mix is QQQ 5 / IEMG 70 / SGOV 20 / BMNR 5. That answer is rejected. It over-fits a point estimate whose two least reliable inputs — the valuation reversion and the currency drag — are precisely what drive it, and it concentrates a dollar investor in a bloc where China alone is roughly a quarter of the index. Capping the tilt at 40% keeps most of the benefit while leaving the conclusion robust to those assumptions being wrong.
SGOV stays at 25% as ammunition, not as fear. With a composite reading of 4.8/10, implied volatility 24% below its own historical average and the 10-year at post-2023 highs, a repricing is a live risk. Holding the reserve in 0–3 month bills rather than long bonds means it is not itself damaged by rising yields, and it earns 3.63% while waiting. Its function is to be sold into the drawdown to rebalance back to target — a 25% reserve is what converts a −27% drawdown from a loss into a buying opportunity.
BMNR is capped at 5% because of risk, not weight. At a twentieth of the capital it contributes 23.8% of portfolio variance — a 4.8× risk-to-weight ratio. It is also a poor diversifier: 0.65 correlated to QQQ, it fails in the same regimes as the growth sleeve. Verification did improve its standing — the premium is 1.02× on total NAV of $15.6B, not the 1.10× that crypto holdings alone imply — but with severe dilution history and an 80.5% drawdown already on the tape, 5% remains the largest defensible position. It earns its place as convexity to an ETH recovery, sized so total loss costs 5% of capital.
Fees are a controlled variable. Weighted expense falls from 0.126% to 0.112% purely as a by-product of the rotation, since IEMG charges 0.09% against QQQ’s 0.18%. Over ten years that is $24 saved per $10,000 — small, but return taken with no risk added. QQQ’s own cut from 0.20% is worth about $41 per $10,000 over the same period.
Recommendation. Adopt the optimized allocation — QQQ 30 / IEMG 40 / SGOV 25 / BMNR 5 — and rebalance to target semi-annually or whenever any sleeve drifts more than 5 points. Rebalancing is doing most of the work here: it mechanically trims the sleeve that has run and buys the one that has fallen, which is what turns BMNR’s 95% volatility from a liability into a small, systematic source of return. Revisit the tilt if the Fed hikes rather than holds, if the 10-year breaks decisively above 5%, or if the EM forward-multiple discount narrows from 40% back toward its 25% long-run norm — that last one is the thesis, and its closing is the signal to take the overweight off.
